Transit Speedrunning Rules


These rules are general. Some systems need small changes, which we list on each system's page. The rules exist to keep competition fair and healthy, and we hope you don't find them limiting.

We recommend reading the category rules and system page before you run. For new categories or questions, ask us on our Discord or at transitrunsstaff@gmail.com. We would much rather help you ahead of time than reject a run over a misunderstanding.

For new systems, follow the rules as closely as possible. If a conflict occurs in your planning, we would be happy to assist!

Last Updated 11 October 2026

1. Definitions
TermMeaning
StationA physical station or stop where you can board a vehicle, as shown by an official agency source.
VisitBeing aboard a vehicle that stops at a station and opens its doors, or boarding/alighting at said stop. See rule 2.2.
Public serviceThe system's normal passenger service, open to anyone with a valid fare.
TransferA change between two vehicles, or a change of service pattern on the same vehicle. Any travel on foot, and turning back on the same vehicle at the end of a line, are both transfers.
NodesThe start station, the end station, and all transfer points.
SegmentsThe pieces we use to construct your route. A new segment starts when you get off a vehicle, or reach a new station on foot.
Track sectionA piece of track on the map between two junctions, or between a junction and the end of a line. Used only for maps without stop icons (rule 3.4).
CategoryThe set of stations (or track) a run must cover, such as All Stops. Each system's page lists potential categories.

Nodes and segments

Each line is a segment. Each dot is a node: the start, the end, or a transfer.

A walk between stations is its own segment.

2. The Basics

Visit every station in your category, aboard the system's own vehicles, with the chance to board or alight at every stop.

  1. To complete a run, you must visit every station in your category.
    1. You don't have to visit stations that open only on some days, such as event stations. If a station has at least one scheduled service every day of the week, you must visit it, even if only available for a few hours.
    2. If some stations or lines close on certain days as a matter of course (such as stations closed at weekends), we may offer a category for those days. See our GO Train leaderboard for an example.
  1. To visit a station, you must be aboard or boarding a vehicle of the system you are running, in public service, arriving at or leaving from the station.
    1. You only need to visit each station once, on one line, at one platform. Section 3 has more details on what counts as one station.
    2. You are not required to get off. Passing through on a vehicle is a valid visit.
    3. Stations that express, limited or skip-stop services pass without stopping do not count. The doors must be able to open for any intending passengers.
    4. If a station is a request stop, the vehicle doesn't need to stop or open its doors.
  1. If something unusual prevents a vehicle from calling at one or more stations:
    1. If your vehicle passes through a mid-line station where it would normally stop, the station counts as visited.
    2. If the agency runs replacement buses, or names existing bus routes as replacements, use these to visit the closed stations.
    3. Otherwise, visit the stations by another approved way of travel (Section 5).

Closures

If your train passes through a closed station where it would normally stop, the station counts (rule 2.3a).

If no trains reach a station, use the replacement bus (rule 2.3b), or another approved way of travel (rule 2.3c).

  1. During a run, you may only travel in the ways listed in Section 5.
  2. A run is one attempt from start to finish. You may pause only as Section 6 sets out, and the timer never stops between the start and end of your run.
3. How do I decide what to run?

Official maps published by agencies decide which stops you are required to visit, and in which directions.

If your category page contains a map, use that one. Category page rules override general ones.

Agency MapAction TakenRule
One line, one icon per stopVisit each stop once, either direction3.1
Two lines, stops paired across linesVisit the pair once, on any side3.2
Two lines, stops not pairedVisit every stop on both sides3.3
Lines, but no stop iconsRide every track section3.4

3.1 One line, single stops

The map draws one line, with one icon for each stop.

Visit each stop once, in either direction.

Example: Muni Metro

3.2 Two lines, paired stops

The map draws each direction as its own line, but links the stops on both sides with one icon, a connector, or one shared name.

Visit each pair once, on either side. You only need to travel one way.

3.3 Two lines, unpaired stops

The map draws each direction as its own line, and the stops on each side stand alone.

Visit every stop on both sides.

3.4 No stop icons

The map shows lines but no stops.

Ride every unique section of track on the map, end to end. Either direction counts, unless the track runs one way.

Examples: Toronto, Portland

  1. Where the two directions of a line run on different streets or tracks with no stops drawn (rule 3.4), each one is its own track section. Ride both.
  2. On maps with no stop icons, the clock still starts when the doors close at your first stop. It stops when the doors open at the first stop after your last track section.
  3. If one map mixes these cases, apply the matching rule to each part.
  4. If you can't tell which case applies, ask us before you run.

4. Special Stations & One-Offs

Multi-agency stations & special cases.

One-way station

Trains stop in one direction only.

You must visit on a train in a direction that will stop at that station.

Examples: Aqueduct Racetrack (New York), Mirabeau (Paris Line 10)

One-way loop

Trains run round a loop in one direction.

You must ride around the loop in full. Each loop stop needs its own visit. Staying aboard round the loop is not a transfer.

Examples: Paris Line 10 (Auteuil loop), Paris Line 7bis

Terminal loops

The last stop and the first stop of a loop can sit on different streets.

They count as one stop. Visit either one.

Example: L Taraval (Muni Metro): 47th & Cutler and Wawona & 46th

A station only counts when your train stops there, or could stop there for a request.

5. Getting Around

Between vehicles, walk, run or take public transport. Nothing private.

Allowed

  • Walking and running
  • Wheelchairs and mobility scooters
  • Piggyback rides
  • Lifts, escalators and moving walkways
  • Public buses, trains, trams, streetcars and shuttles
  • Public ferries, cable cars and funiculars
  • Scheduled commercial flights
  • Jet Skis, as long as you return to place of origin with no intermediary travel.

Not allowed

  • Bicycles, including bikeshare
  • Scooters, skateboards and other small vehicles
  • Private cars, taxis and ride-hailing
  • Paratransit and chartered vehicles
  • Private boats (excluding jet skis) and aircraft
  • This is a non exhaustive list.
  1. Only the system currently being run is able to visit stations. Other methods of transport are only for intermediary travel.
  2. Bikeshare & bicycles are prohibited due to unequal access. Bikeshare isn't universally accessible, and in some places is run by private operators. We allow running because agencies often expect riders to walk between stations.
  3. We may still reject a run with very long distances on foot.
  4. These lists are not exhaustive. If you have a question, just ask!
  5. During a break (Section 6), you may travel any way you like.
6. Breaks

You may pause, but the clock is still active and you must return back to where your break started.

We expect most runs to be done in one go, but you may take breaks, such as to sleep on a run that spans several days. If you do:

  1. Keep the clock running. If you use a stopwatch, it must not stop during the break.
  2. Start again from the exact place you stopped.
  3. Show in your footage when the break starts and ends, and that you are in the same place both times.
  4. You may do whatever you like during the break, including travel by banned means, and you don't need to film it.
  5. You cannot visit any stations during the break.

How a break works

The stopwatch never stops, and no stations count while you are on a break.

Film the start and end of the break, in the same place.

7. Team Runs

Teams travel together. Only members present for the entirety of the run, to all stations, will receive credit.

  1. One team member submits the run and lists everyone who took part. Multiple submissions will not decrease verification time.
  2. Only people present for the entirety of the run, which includes all stations, will be mentioned.
  3. The team must visit each station together. A station counts only when every credited runner is aboard. You may not split up to cover different stations.
  4. People who drop out or join partway receive no credit. Additionally, since team runs are judged as if run by one person, you may not take shifts to trade off the running camera.
8. Conduct & Safety

No record is worth breaking the law for. Runs that break our standards of conduct may be rejected.

  1. Pay every fare and always carry a valid ticket.
  2. Obey the law, the agency's rules and its staff.
  3. Never enter tracks, tunnels or staff-only areas, hold doors, or ride on the outside of a vehicle.
  4. When on foot, use pavements and paths, and use prudent judgement when crossing streets. Don't run where you could hurt someone, such as on crowded platforms or stairs.
  5. Don't get in the way of service or other riders.
  6. Follow local rules on filming, and respect people who don't want to be filmed.
  7. Be courteous to all members of the public and to TransitRuns community members, online and offline.
9. Submission & Evidence

Our rule: We must be able to retrace your entire route.

  1. You need an active TransitRuns account to submit a run. For team runs, only one account should submit (Section 7).
  2. You must submit:
    1. Who else took part (with TransitRuns usernames, if they have them);
    2. Which system you ran, and which category;
    3. The date of the run;
    4. The local time the run began;
    5. The length of the run in HH:MM:SS format, unless you follow a timing standard in Section 10;
    6. Sufficient evidence to validate your run, usually a piece of key evidence; and
    7. Any other comments, including service problems that affected your run.
  1. Your key evidence must meet these standards:
    1. It must let us retrace your whole route, making every segment clear: which lines and stations you used, and when.
    2. Good evidence includes:
      1. Video of parts of the run, above all at nodes (transfer points); or
      2. Photos with unedited digital timestamps, taken at stations.
    3. We suggest footage that shows both the stopwatch and the local time (such as a departure board or clock), above all at transfers.
    4. Photos and video must make clear which station you are at: a station sign, an on-board screen, an announcement you can hear, or other features we can identify.
    5. On maps without stop icons (rule 3.4), your evidence must also show that you rode each track section from end to end.

Additionally, you MUST submit a log of your run, detailing transfer times and your route, as well as the evidence for each segment.

10. Timing

The clock starts when the doors close at your first station and stops when they open at the last.

FinishWhen the doors begin to open at your last station. Not when you step onto the platform.
BreaksThe clock keeps running (Section 6).
RetimingWe reserve the right to retime your run from your footage to match the start and finish above.

11. Changes & Contact
  1. We reserve the right to change our rules at any time, and apply them at our discretion. When we do, we update the date at the top of this page.
  2. We judge each run by the rules in force on the day it was run.
  3. Questions, or a case these rules don't cover? Ask us on our Discord or at transitrunsstaff@gmail.com.
